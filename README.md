## About This Project

This project is a **Business Analysis simulation** focused on understanding how changing customer expectations and the growing adoption of fintech solutions may influence customer engagement with traditional banking services.

The case compares **ABB**, a large universal bank offering retail, corporate, SME, lending, savings, payment and digital banking services, with **m10**, a mobile-first digital wallet focused primarily on everyday payments, instant peer-to-peer transfers, QR payments and simplified financial transactions.

The project investigates the potential relationship between **customer churn and fintech adoption**. The main business question is:

> **Why might customers reduce their engagement with traditional banking services and increasingly adopt mobile-first fintech solutions for their everyday financial activities?**

Using a combination of **synthetic customer and transaction data, business analysis techniques and data visualization**, the project explores customer behavior, identifies potential churn drivers, analyzes digital adoption patterns and develops recommendations that could help a traditional financial institution improve customer retention and digital engagement.

This is an **educational portfolio simulation**. The analysis does not use confidential customer data from ABB or m10, and the findings should not be interpreted as actual internal performance results of either organization.

### Why This Project?

The financial services industry is becoming increasingly customer-centric and digital. Customers can now choose between traditional banks, mobile banking applications and specialized fintech platforms depending on their needs and preferred experience.

ABB's profile demonstrates the scale and breadth of a universal banking model, while m10 represents a more focused mobile-first approach centered around speed, convenience and simple daily transactions. This creates an interesting Business Analysis scenario: **how can a traditional financial institution identify changing customer needs and reduce the risk of customers moving their everyday financial activity toward specialized fintech alternatives?**

This project was created to demonstrate how a Business Analyst can combine **business context, stakeholder analysis, process analysis, requirements engineering and data analysis** to investigate a realistic business problem and support evidence-based decision-making.

## Key Findings

### 1. Customer Expectations Are Moving Toward Speed and Convenience

The comparison highlights a difference between a **broad universal banking model** and a **specialized mobile-first payment experience**. ABB provides a wide range of products and services, while m10's presented value proposition focuses heavily on fast, simple and convenient everyday transactions.

This suggests that customers may not necessarily leave traditional banks entirely. Instead, they may begin using fintech platforms for specific high-frequency activities such as transfers, QR payments, utility payments and other daily transactions.

### 2. Everyday Transaction Experience Can Influence Customer Engagement

m10's customer experience is designed around a small number of frequently used actions, including **transfers, QR payments and top-ups**, with these functions prioritized within the mobile experience.

A key project hypothesis is that reducing friction in high-frequency customer journeys could improve digital engagement. If customers find everyday transactions faster and easier through an alternative platform, their usage of traditional banking channels may gradually decrease.

### 3. Traditional Banks Have Scale, Trust and Product Breadth — but Must Continue Improving Digital Experience

ABB's project profile represents a large financial institution with millions of individual customers, extensive service infrastructure and a broad portfolio covering cards, loans, deposits, payments and business banking.

This scale is a significant advantage. However, the analysis indicates that product breadth alone may not guarantee strong engagement in a digital-first market. Customers increasingly evaluate financial services based on the quality of individual experiences, particularly for tasks they perform frequently.

### 4. Churn Should Be Viewed as a Behavioral Process

For this project, churn is not treated only as a customer closing an account. A customer may remain officially connected to a traditional bank while reducing transaction frequency, shifting payment activity or adopting another platform as their primary tool for daily financial activity.

This makes **early behavioral indicators** important. Changes in transaction frequency, digital activity and fintech adoption can potentially provide signals before complete disengagement occurs.

### 5. Customer Retention Requires More Than Traditional Loyalty

The project suggests that retention strategies should focus not only on keeping customers registered but also on keeping them **actively engaged**.

Potential areas for improvement include:

* Simplifying high-frequency digital journeys
* Identifying customers with declining engagement
* Using behavioral data to detect potential churn risk
* Improving personalization
* Increasing the value of digital loyalty and rewards
* Creating faster and more seamless payment experiences

### 6. Data Can Support Earlier and More Targeted Business Decisions

By combining customer behavior, transaction activity and fintech adoption indicators, the proposed analysis framework enables a more proactive approach to retention.

Rather than waiting until customers become inactive, business teams could use data-driven indicators to identify segments with changing behavior and design targeted actions based on their needs.
